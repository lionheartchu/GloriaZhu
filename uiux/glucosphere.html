Gluco
Sphere

A guided glucose-testing kit for children, paired with an app for parents.

GlucoPal is the child-facing physical kit: its screen breaks a finger-stick test into visible steps while the case keeps tools in fixed locations. Gluardian is the caregiver app: it receives each completed test result, then organizes records and trends.

02. Why it matters

1.21M

Children and adolescents under 20 living with type 1 diabetes worldwide.

IDF Diabetes Atlas, 10th edition · 2021

Designed for adults, handed to children

Type 2 incidence among young people doubled across the study period. The brief narrowed to children aged 6 to 12 — old enough to run the test, young enough that a clinical device reads as frightening. A review of market leaders including Care Touch and Abbott found the same four frictions repeated.

  • Clinical appearance. Reads as medical equipment.
  • Pain and resistance. Fingersticks hurt, so testing gets refused.
  • Unclear feedback. A bare number means nothing to a child.
  • Social stigma. Visible testing gets skipped at school.
Design opportunity

How might a glucose test become something a child starts on their own? Reframe a clinical routine as a guided, rewarded task — a kit that does not announce itself as medical, paired with a caregiver view that makes consistency visible.

04. From needs to design

From sequence to form

The process material tests three connected questions: how the case opens and stores tools, how the child moves through the test, and how the early wireframes divide information between screens.

  1. 01Keep the sequence visible. Show one step at a time, name the tool, and tie each prompt to a physical control.
  2. 02Make completion explicit. Confirm the test is finished, show its timestamp, and place the reading in history.
  3. 03Separate child and caregiver tasks. The child’s flow stays on the procedure; the caregiver view supports review.
